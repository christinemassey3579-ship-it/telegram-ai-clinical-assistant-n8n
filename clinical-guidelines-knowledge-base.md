# Clinical Guidelines – Sample Knowledge Base

Sample data loaded into the assistant's RAG knowledge base. **For demonstration only – not medical advice.**

## 1. Type 2 Diabetes – Metformin

**Indication:** First-line drug therapy for type 2 diabetes alongside diet and exercise.

**Dosage (adults):**
- Immediate-release: start 500 mg once or twice daily with meals (or 850 mg once daily).
- Increase by 500 mg per week as tolerated to reduce stomach upset.
- Usual maintenance: 1,500–2,000 mg per day in divided doses. Maximum 2,550 mg per day.
- Extended-release: start 500–1,000 mg once daily with the evening meal; maximum 2,000 mg per day.

**Kidney function (eGFR):**
- eGFR below 30: do not use.
- eGFR 30–45: do not start; if already taking, review benefit and consider dose reduction.
- Check eGFR before starting and at least once a year.
- Pause before iodinated contrast if eGFR is 30–60; restart after 48 hours once kidney function is stable.

**Side effects:** nausea, diarrhoea, metallic taste (usually settle with slow increase); vitamin B12 deficiency with long-term use; lactic acidosis (rare, serious).

**Monitoring:** HbA1c every 3 months until at target, then every 6 months. Usual HbA1c target below 7% for most adults (individualise for older or frail patients).

## 2. Hypertension – Treatment Guideline

**Diagnosis:** Confirm with repeated readings or home/ambulatory monitoring.

**Target:** below 130/80 mmHg for most adults; less strict targets may suit frail older patients.

**First-line medicines:** ACE inhibitor or ARB, calcium-channel blocker, or thiazide-type diuretic.

**Amlodipine:** start 5 mg once daily (2.5 mg in older adults or liver impairment). Maximum 10 mg once daily. Common side effect: ankle swelling.

**Lisinopril:** start 10 mg once daily (5 mg if already on a diuretic or kidney impairment). Usual 20–40 mg once daily; maximum 80 mg. Check potassium and creatinine 1–2 weeks after starting or increasing. Stop if angioedema. Avoid in pregnancy.

**Follow-up:** review 2–4 weeks after any medicine change; monthly until at target, then every 3–6 months.

## 3. High Cholesterol – Atorvastatin

- Moderate intensity: 10–20 mg once daily.
- High intensity (established heart disease or high risk): 40–80 mg once daily.
- Check lipid panel 4–12 weeks after starting or changing dose, then every 3–12 months.
- Ask about muscle pain; check CK if symptoms. Check liver tests at baseline.
- Avoid in pregnancy.

## 4. Hypothyroidism – Levothyroxine

- Full replacement: about 1.6 mcg per kg per day.
- Older adults or heart disease: start 25–50 mcg daily and increase slowly.
- Take on an empty stomach, 30–60 minutes before breakfast; separate from calcium and iron by 4 hours.
- Check TSH 6–8 weeks after starting or changing the dose, then yearly once stable.

## 5. Pain and Fever

**Paracetamol (acetaminophen):** 500–1,000 mg every 4–6 hours as needed. Maximum 4 g per day; maximum 3 g per day (or lower) in liver disease, heavy alcohol use or low body weight.

**Ibuprofen:** 200–400 mg every 4–6 hours with food. Over-the-counter maximum 1,200 mg per day; prescription maximum 3,200 mg per day. Avoid in active stomach ulcer, significant kidney disease, late pregnancy, or with anticoagulants unless a clinician advises.

## 6. Uncomplicated Urinary Tract Infection (non-pregnant adult women)

- Nitrofurantoin 100 mg (modified release) twice daily for 5 days. Avoid if eGFR below 30.
- Alternative per local resistance: trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole 160/800 mg twice daily for 3 days.
- Escalate if fever, flank pain, vomiting, pregnancy, or symptoms in men (these are not "uncomplicated").

## 7. Community-Acquired Pneumonia (outpatient, healthy adult, no risk factors)

- Amoxicillin 1 g three times daily for 5 days (minimum), or doxycycline 100 mg twice daily.
- Assess severity (e.g., CURB-65). Refer to hospital if confusion, low oxygen saturation, low blood pressure, or high respiratory rate.
- Review at 48–72 hours if not improving.

## 8. Asthma – Reliever Inhaler

- Salbutamol (albuterol) 100 mcg per puff: 1–2 puffs every 4–6 hours as needed.
- Using a reliever more than twice a week means asthma is poorly controlled: review inhaler technique and controller therapy.
- Emergency: if unable to speak in full sentences, oxygen saturation below 92%, or no response to reliever, call emergency services.

## 9. Follow-up and Scheduling Protocols

| Situation | Follow-up timing |
|---|---|
| New metformin start or dose change | 2–4 weeks (tolerance), HbA1c at 3 months |
| Blood pressure medicine change | 2–4 weeks |
| Post-hospital discharge | Within 7 days |
| New levothyroxine dose | TSH at 6–8 weeks |
| New statin | Lipids at 4–12 weeks |
| Antibiotic course not improving | 48–72 hours |
| Routine stable chronic disease | Every 3–6 months |

- Standard follow-up appointment length: 30 minutes. New patient: 45 minutes.
- Clinic hours: Monday–Saturday, 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM IST.

## 10. Lab Results Workflow

- Upload all lab reports to the patient record within 24 hours of receipt.
- Clinician reviews routine results within 48 hours.
- Critical values (e.g., potassium above 6.0 or below 2.5 mmol/L, glucose below 3.0 mmol/L / 54 mg/dL) must be phoned to the treating doctor the same day and documented.

## 11. Doctor Summary Format (for emails)

Use this structure when sending a patient summary to a doctor:
1. Patient name / ID and date
2. Reason for contact
3. Key findings (vitals, labs)
4. Current medicines and doses
5. Actions taken
6. Questions or requests for the doctor
7. Follow-up planned

Do not include more personal data than the doctor needs.

## 12. Red Flags – Escalate Immediately

Chest pain, stroke symptoms (face drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty), severe breathing difficulty, signs of anaphylaxis, blood glucose below 3.0 mmol/L with confusion, suicidal thoughts, or any rapidly worsening condition: advise emergency care (India: dial 112 / 108) and inform the treating doctor.
